# English for Dutch speakers (`en_from_nl`): curriculum

This is the plan for the 59 units of the English course. The explanations are in Dutch, and the
examples and exercises are in British English. American spelling is accepted with a warning.
The levels follow the CEFR. The exams the course prepares for are the Cambridge English
Qualifications: A2 Key, B1 Preliminary, B2 First, C1 Advanced and C2 Proficiency.

The units use the same building blocks as the Spanish course: tables, verb tables (conj), word
pairs, mistakes, exercises and translations. Unit files are called
`content/<level>_<nn>_<topic>.json`, and their PDFs are made by `make_pdfs.py`. Every unit ends
with a self-check and a short translation exercise, and has a "Nederlands" callout. That callout
explains where Dutch and English differ, because that is where Dutch speakers make mistakes.

Main trouble spots for Dutch speakers. These come back again and again in the units:
- **Word order.** English has no verb-second rule and no verb at the end of the clause:
  *Yesterday I saw him*, not *Yesterday saw I him*.
- **Tense choice.** The present perfect and the past simple do not match *ik heb ... gedaan*.
  The continuous forms have no direct Dutch equivalent.
- **do-support** in questions and negatives (*Do you know ...?*).
- **Pronunciation.** th, w/v, final voiced consonants, word stress, and silent letters.
- **False friends**: *eventually*, *actual*, *sensible*, *brave*, *become*.
- **Prepositions and phrasal verbs.**

## Also in the course

  to fill in, as in the Spanish course.

- **Model exams** (step 3.9): links to the official Cambridge sample papers for A2 Key,
  B1 Preliminary, B2 First, C1 Advanced and C2 Proficiency.

## Engine changes this course needs

- English has no grammatical gender, so the "de or het?" / "el or la?" exercise is left out.
  This already works through the language profile (gender None).
- English answers may use contractions (*don't* = *do not*). This is handled by `expand()` in
  `languages.py`.
- American spelling is accepted with a warning (`spelling` warning kind).
