# Dutch for English speakers (`nl_from_en`): curriculum

This is the plan for the 59 units of the Dutch course. The explanations are in English; the
examples and exercises are in the Dutch of the Netherlands (Belgian words and usage are mentioned
where they differ). The levels follow the CEFR. The exams the course prepares for:

- **A2**: the Dutch integration exam (Inburgeringsexamen, A2) and CNaVT A2 (Profiel Maatschappelijk Informeel).
- **B1**: Staatsexamen NT2, Programma I, and CNaVT B1 (Profiel Maatschappelijk Formeel).
- **B2**: Staatsexamen NT2, Programma II, and CNaVT B2 (Profiel Zakelijk Professioneel, Profiel Hoger Onderwijs).
- **C1**: CNaVT C1 (Profiel Academische Taalvaardigheid, PAT) and the Staatsexamen NT2 C1.
- **C2**: there is no general C2 exam; the course aims at near-native reading and writing.

The units use the same building blocks as the other courses: tables, verb tables (conj), word
pairs, mistakes, exercises and translations. Unit files are called
`content/<level>_<nn>_<topic>.json`, and their PDFs are made by `make_pdfs.py`. Every unit ends
with a self-check and a short translation exercise, and has an "English" callout that explains
where Dutch and English differ.

Main trouble spots for English speakers. These come back again and again in the units:
- **Word order.** The finite verb comes second in a main clause (*Morgen ga ik*), at the end of a
  subordinate clause (*omdat ik moe ben*), and infinitives and participles go to the end
  (*Ik heb het boek gelezen*).
- **de or het.** Every noun has an article to learn; adjective endings (-e or not) depend on it.
- **Separable verbs**: *opbellen → Ik bel je op*.
- **Perfect tense with hebben or zijn**, and the strong verbs (*lopen – liep – gelopen*).
- **Pronunciation.** g/ch, ui, eu, ij/ei, the short and long vowels (*man / maan*), and the
  spelling rules that follow from them (*bomen, bommen*).
- **The little words**: *er*, modal particles (*even, maar, toch, wel, nou*), and *zich*.
- **False friends**: *eventueel, actueel, brutaal, slim, bellen, durven*.

## A1 (9 units)

| Unit | Title | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| a1_01 | Pronunciation and spelling | alphabet, short and long vowels, g/ch, ui, eu, ij/ei, open and closed syllables, numbers 0–100 |
| a1_02 | zijn and hebben | ik ben / heb, subject pronouns (jij/u), introducing yourself, nationality, age |
| a1_03 | Present tense | stem + t, jij-inversion (*Werk jij?*), spelling changes (*leven → leef*), yes/no questions |
| a1_04 | de and het | articles, plurals in -en and -s, diminutives in -je (always het) |
| a1_05 | Word order 1 | verb in second place, inversion after time words, questions with question words, *niet* and *geen* |
| a1_06 | Pronouns and possessives | mij/me, hem, haar; mijn, jouw, zijn, haar, ons/onze; *van mij* |
| a1_07 | Modal verbs and separable verbs | kunnen, moeten, willen, mogen; infinitive at the end; *opbellen, meegaan* |
| a1_08 | Adjectives | the -e ending (*een groot huis, het grote huis*), comparison basics |
| a1_09 | Vocabulary and phrases | greetings, family, food, time, days and months, shopping, directions, the classroom |

## Also in the course

- **Model exams** (step 5.9): links to the official practice exams of the Staatsexamen NT2,
  the integration exam and the CNaVT.

  already has `gender: ["de", "het"]`.
